Attack Resistance Is Only Half the Answer:
Evaluating Search-Agent Reliability

Yiwen Lu

University of Notre Dame · ylu37@nd.edu

Working manuscript · October 2026

PaperCode & dataBibTeX

Abstract

A search agent can avoid an attacker's requested answer and still fail the user's question. We examine this gap in a Qwen2.5-3B Search-R1 agent trained with GRPO, using auxiliary answer supervision as a practical probe. Five training configurations are evaluated across three seeds on 1,000 held-out HotpotQA questions under clean, malicious, and benign retrieval conditions. Exposed runs match consumed-attack quotas and a measured loop-time budget, with remaining cost differences reported. Random injection with answer supervision achieves 33.37% attacked exact match versus 23.30% for injection alone, while attack-target hit falls from 21.28% to 2.67% and clean accuracy also improves. Paired outputs reveal what the aggregate gain leaves unresolved: among baseline target-hit cases, more supervised outputs are other wrong answers than correct answers. Trace analysis distinguishes preserved correctness, non-target errors, and regressions, while positional stress tests expose limited transfer. A subsequent audit found inconsistent training-to-inference weight sharing, so the results characterize historical exported policies without isolating supervision's causal effect. The study connects attack resistance to the user's task through joint outcome, exposure, and cost accounting, and identifies the evidence-use questions that remain before claiming reliable recovery.

Scope and open questions

↗

What improved

Attacked exact match improves in all three primary seeds. Clean accuracy also rises and format failures fall. All five configurations and all runs remain visible.

↔

What remains open

Exposure quotas and loop time do not equalize supervision dose, evidence, or total computation. Training/export weight sharing requires a corrected replication before causal attribution.

→

Where it leads

Positional stress tests and exploratory recovery diagnostics motivate a next question: can an agent identify missing evidence and spend its search budget usefully?

Experimental scope

The primary study covers one 3B model family, a local HotpotQA distractor pool, five training configurations, three seeds, and two test attack templates. Its 75,000 trajectories repeat 1,000 questions across models and conditions.

The separate extension uses another 1,000-question set. Later recovery diagnostics reuse development or selected training cases with a frozen author checkpoint; they do not establish why answer supervision improves the historical exports.

This is a working manuscript, not an accepted or independently peer-reviewed paper. The paper includes the full assistance disclosure and experimental qualifications.
