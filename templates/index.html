Abstract

A reliable search agent must still answer the user's question when retrieved text tries to redirect it. We measure both halves of this requirement in Search-R1, with outcome, exposure, and cost accounting. On 1,000 held-out HotpotQA questions, five Qwen2.5-3B training configurations across three seeds compare GRPO with and without auxiliary answer supervision. Random injection with supervision reaches 33.37% attacked exact match versus 23.30% for injection alone, while target hit falls from 21.28% to 2.67%. Paired outputs on the same questions show what this average contains: 931 answers turn from wrong to correct, but 736 of the baseline's 1,277 target hits become a different wrong answer, and 327 answers that were correct regress. Three saved traces show each kind. Development evaluation of author 3B and 7B checkpoints adds that the 7B model rarely emits the target yet still loses accuracy under an override instruction, and that at its own second-search states new retrieval outperforms repeated evidence by 9.4–11.9 points. A weight-sharing audit limits the training comparison to export-level evidence, so a corrected rerun is the next step before the answer loss can be credited. Reliable search is measured by the answer delivered and the evidence acquired, not by target avoidance alone.

How the study is built

Three analyses, each taking the previous one apart.

01

Measure both.

Attacked accuracy and attack-target hit on the same 1,000 questions, for five training configurations and three seeds, with clean and benign controls, consumed exposure, and realized training cost.

02

Pair the answers.

The 6,000 paired answers of the two primary configurations sort into three kinds of change: wrong to correct, target to another wrong answer, and correct to wrong. Three saved traces show each kind.

03

Ask what helps next.

Author 3B and 7B checkpoints on 256 development questions, and a paired test of whether another search brings evidence the agent can use.

Scope and next steps

↗

What is established

Attacked exact match improves in all three primary seeds for the supervised export, clean accuracy rises, and format failures fall; the paired outputs show which answers changed and how. All five configurations and all runs remain visible.

↔

What must be rerun

A later audit found inconsistent training-to-inference weight sharing in the historical pipeline, so the training comparison describes the evaluated exports. Crediting the answer loss causally needs the core comparison rerun on a corrected pipeline. Exposure quotas and loop time also do not equalize supervision dose, evidence, or total computation.

→

Where it leads

A checkpoint that rarely follows the target still loses accuracy, and new retrieval helps at its observed second-search states. The next question is whether an agent can identify missing evidence and spend its search budget usefully.

Experimental scope

The primary study covers one 3B model family, a local HotpotQA distractor pool, five training configurations, three seeds, and two test attack templates. Its 75,000 trajectories repeat 1,000 questions across models and conditions; the paired analysis re-scores saved trajectories without new inference.

The separate extension uses another 1,000-question set. The author-checkpoint comparison adds 7B within the same family, and its evidence intervention uses 256 opened development questions. These do not establish why supervision improves the historical exports or transfer of the training method; the 7B training smoke has not yet passed.
